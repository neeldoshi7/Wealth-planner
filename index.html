Tax on complete exit

Capital gains are taxed at the long-term rate shown, on the gain (value minus amount invested) after the yearly exemption (₹1.25 L under Section 112A), deducted once since everything is sold in one year. Bond interest is taxed at the client's income slab rate, with no exemption; in practice it is taxed each year as it is earned, so it is shown here as a total. Debt funds bought after 1 April 2023 and gains held under a year are also taxed differently; check with a tax adviser.

Actual returns depend on market conditions and may be lower or negative, in which case the money may run out sooner. Each fund grows at its own expected return, compounding monthly for monthly payouts and yearly for yearly payouts; each year, mutual funds and SIFs pay out the withdrawal % of their value at the start of that year, and bonds pay out the withdrawal % of the amount first invested in them; the yearly amount is paid in equal parts at the end of each month (or once at year end) by each fund. Bond values grow at the yield entered, with coupons kept in the portfolio and taxed as they are earned. Tax is long-term capital gains tax at the rate shown on the gains part of each withdrawal from year 2, worked out on average cost; year 1 withdrawals are shown without tax. Each plan year, the first ₹1.25 L of gains (the Section 112A exemption, editable above) is tax-free, so tax starts once a year's gains pass it; plan years are treated as tax years. Bond interest is taxed at the income slab rate entered, every year as it is earned (including year 1), with no exemption. Debt funds bought after 1 April 2023 are also taxed at slab rates; check with a tax adviser. Figures are before exit loads and fees. Investments in mutual funds, SIFs and bonds are subject to market risks; read all scheme and offer documents carefully.
